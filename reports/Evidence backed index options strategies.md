# Slow, cheap signals beat index-options scalping

**Bottom line: no published, credible evidence supports a minute-scale directional options scalper on QQQ, SPY or IWM, and your own replay is consistent with that.** The best-evidenced strategy families are slow (monthly to once-a-day), are documented on shares or futures rather than options, and survive costs only because their per-trade costs are one to two basis points of the underlying. Options are a wrapper that has to earn its place. On the evidence, only one family has a structural, options-native rationale: defined-risk premium selling at 30-45 days to expiry, which harvests the variance risk premium, though no source I could verify tests that exact form net of costs. The ranked shortlist is: (1) closing-hour momentum, the best-published intraday signal, run on SPY shares or micro futures with options only shadow-logged; (2) a monthly trend and volatility gate that sizes and permits everything else; (3) a regime-gated put credit spread sleeve at 30-45 DTE; (4) a once-a-day noise-area breakout, shares first; and then research-only or discard items. Your 690-trade replay lost about $28,600 against roughly $21,400 of modeled spread, so almost all of the loss is cost and the bots carry roughly zero gross edge. That is what the retail evidence predicts: a placebo that lost about $20K shows the "signal" bots were statistically close to random direction. Published edges also shrink: expect 58% lower returns post-publication, and plan on a live Sharpe of about 0.4 times the published figure. At that haircut the shortlist needs 7 to 16 years of data to prove itself, so validation must lean on long historical tests and treat paper trading as a falsifier of costs and execution, not a confirmer of alpha. Sourced figures carry citations below. Everything marked (derived) or (judgement) is my own arithmetic or design choice, not published evidence.

## Your replay lost to spread, and the placebo shows why

Your test: ATM 1DTE options, one-minute VWAP/EMA50/opening-range bots, Aug-Sep 2026, $0.01-0.02 modeled spread per side, 10 contracts, 690 trades, about -$28,600, about $31 of spread per trade, random-direction placebo about -$20K. These are your results, not published evidence. The arithmetic ties together (derived): 690 trades at $31 is about $21,400 of spread, so gross P&L was about -$7,200, or about -$10 a trade, which is noise next to a $31 cost. The placebo losing about $20K, close to the pure spread bill, says a random-direction coin flip does about as well as your signals before cost. A 1 ATR stop with a 2 ATR target has a gross breakeven win rate of one third, so near-chance win rates mean zero gross expectancy. The video-style exits lifted the pre-spread result by about $11K, which on 690 trades is roughly +$16 a trade, still only about half of the $31 spread, and about 6x short of the 3x-cost rule below (derived: a $93 gross edge per trade would be needed). In R-multiple terms with a 1:2 payoff, expectancy is 3p - 1 - c, where p is win rate and c is round-trip cost as a fraction of the risk unit; at c = 0.2R a strategy needs a 40% win rate to break even (derived). Compute c from your own fills as spread divided by (delta times ATR), because that single number tells you whether any minute-scale signal can pay.

The published base rates point the same way. Retail traders in SPX 0DTE options lost more than $70M over about two years, an average of $184K a day and $358K a day once daily expirations began, and more than $50M of that came from transaction costs ([Beckmeyer, Branger and Gayda, SSRN](https://ssrn.com/abstract=4404704); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-04-21/day-traders-lose-358-000-per-day-gambling-on-zero-day-options)). Across stock and futures day traders, 97% of persistent Brazilian futures day traders lost money and fewer than 1% of Taiwanese day traders earned positive net abnormal returns ([FGV repository](https://repositorio.fgv.br/items/e87d04fc-4b4c-4a56-ab5a-1c84c1afde1b/full); [Barber, Lee, Liu and Odean](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)). Those studies measure mostly discretionary high-turnover flow, so they set a cost base rate, not a ceiling for systematic strategies. Cost realism also cuts against your $0.01-0.02 per side assumption. Traders who cannot time quotes pay roughly the conventionally measured effective spread, about 6.2 cents against an 8.1-cent average quoted spread in one study, and only latency-advantaged traders pay a fraction of it ([Muravyev and Pearson, summarized by CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-options/timing-option-trades-to-suppress-trading-frictions/)). Taking the ratio gives an untimed fill near 77% of the quoted spread (derived). Retail API latency forfeits the timing edge, so assume none. Spreads also scale badly with tenor: one search-result snippet puts average spreads up to about 9% for ultra-short-dated options against about 2% for 21-48 DTE ([Volos, via ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/388321185_Profitability_of_selected_0DTE_index_options_strategies)). That figure is low confidence, since it came only from a snippet and I could not check its basis.

The deeper point is that trade frequency multiplies cost while edge per trade stays tiny. Your bots made about 16 trades a day over about 42 sessions (derived). Even the strongest published intraday signal earns a couple of basis points per trade: in the original first-half-hour study on SPY, a 1.2 bp proportional spread cut annual return from about 7.96% to 6.52% after 2005, and cut it from about 6.9% to 4.46% over the post-2001 sample ([Gao, Han, Li and Zhou draft](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/intraday_momentum_-_the_first_half-hour_return_predicts_the_last_half-hour_return.pdf)). Replace that 1.2 bp share spread with a multi-leg option spread that is several times wider as a fraction of underlying move, and the edge disappears. A published example of this kind of collapse: an independent opening-range-breakout replication on five index CFDs found gross edges of +0.12R to +0.13R and net edges from +0.002R (NQ) to -0.08R (SPX, Dow, FTSE) ([MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)). That is a blog, not peer review, and CFD costs exceed futures costs, so it overstates the penalty for a futures trader, but the direction matches your result.

## Three tools decide what is credible: the rubric, the track-record length formula and the deflated Sharpe

Published anomalies decay. McLean and Pontiff found 97 predictors lost 26% of their return out of sample and 58% after publication ([J. Finance 2016, summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/359722)). Replication rates are contested, from 35% to 82% depending on method, but all of that evidence is cross-sectional equity factors, and none directly validates a single-instrument index-options rule ([Hou, Xue and Zhang](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v33y2020i5p2019-2133..html); [Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28432)). New claims should clear t > 3 ([Harvey, Liu and Zhu](https://nber.org/papers/w20592)). I therefore use a planning prior of live Sharpe equal to published Sharpe times 0.4 (judgement; the notes derive it from the 58% decay and Harvey-Liu haircuts, it is not a published number).

For track-record length, the Bailey and Lopez de Prado minimum track record formula is MinTRL = [1 - skew x SR + (kurtosis - 1)/4 x SR^2] x [z / SR]^2 in observations ([reproduced at Portfolio Optimizer](https://portfoliooptimizer.io/blog/the-probabilistic-sharpe-ratio-bias-adjustment-confidence-intervals-hypothesis-testing-and-minimum-track-record-length/)). Under normal returns that reduces to years = (1 + SR^2/2) x (1.645/SR)^2 at 95% confidence. The notes' own computation gives 12.2 years at an annual Sharpe of 0.5, 4.1 years at 1.0, 2.6 at 1.5 and 2.0 at 2.0 (derived). Negative skew and fat tails, which short-premium strategies carry, lengthen this multiplicatively: a skew of -2 and kurtosis of 10 at Sharpe 1.5 needs about 4.3 times more data (derived). For the deflated Sharpe, the benchmark Sharpe after N independent trials is SR* = sqrt(V) x [(1 - g) x Phi^-1(1 - 1/N) + g x Phi^-1(1 - 1/(N e))], with g = 0.5772 ([Bailey and Lopez de Prado, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551); formula as reproduced in [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deflated_Sharpe_ratio)). In Sharpe-estimate standard deviations the expected best result from pure noise is about 1.19 for 5 trials, 2.28 for 50 and 2.53 for 100 (derived). Every parameter value, filter, instrument, DTE, delta and time-of-day you try counts as a trial, including the parameter grid behind your August-September work. Bailey, Borwein, Lopez de Prado and Zhu show that after only 7 configurations a researcher is expected to find a 2-year backtest with annualized Sharpe above 1 when the true Sharpe is zero ([Notices AMS](https://scholarworks.wmich.edu/math_pubs/40/)). The notes could not verify the exact minimum backtest length equation, so I do not use it.

On cost, the notes' working rules are my judgement, not published standards: charge 50-100% of the quoted full spread per leg per round trip plus exchange fees; require gross expected P&L of at least 3x the modeled round-trip cost; require the strategy to stay profitable (Sharpe above 0.5, positive mean) at 2x modeled costs; add a 1-5 second signal delay. An eight-criterion 0-2 rubric ranks the candidates, with hard gates that exclude any rule with no disclosed trial count, no net-of-cost result, evidence only from social media or marketed hypothetical results, or unbounded loss in a sample with no stress period (judgement; the rubric weights have not been validated against any outcome data). Regulators already treat hypothetical and backtested performance as needing special disclosure, which is why screenshots and platform badges score zero here ([Troutman on the SEC Marketing Rule](https://www.troutman.com/insights/the-secs-new-marketing-rule-practically-speaking-hypothetical-performance/)). The Cboe benchmark index histories are mostly backtests before their launch dates, so only their post-launch segments count as live.

## Cost-surviving evidence exists for slow signals on shares and futures, and nowhere for options scalps

The following table summarizes each family. Rubric scores are my judgement from the notes' criteria (maximum 16). Where a score assesses an options form, the evidence for the underlying signal is rated separately, because no paper tested any of these signals with options.

| Family | Best evidence (sourced) | Survives costs in the documented form? | Decay and caveats | Rubric (judgement) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Closing-hour momentum | Baltussen et al., JFE 2021: 60+ futures, 1974-2020; equity-index timing Sharpe 1.73 gross using return from prior close to 30 min before close | Positive net Sharpe for S&P futures at one tick, per authors, no cost model | First-half-hour signal alone fails out of sample (R2 -1.71%); nothing tested after May 2020 | 12 |
| Trend and volatility gate | AQR century of trend: net Sharpe 0.77 (1880-2013), 0.62 (2000-2013); Faber S&P 500 SMA Sharpe 0.55 vs 0.32 | Yes, because turnover is 3-4 trades a year | Zakamulin: net Sharpe gain only 7-12%, benefit is drawdown, not return | 11 |
| Defined-risk premium selling | Cboe PUT index 10.13% vs S&P 9.85% with 10.16% vs 15.26% vol; VRP is peer-reviewed | Index figures are gross; Vilkov iron condor net Sharpe -0.20 (0DTE) | PUT 2022 -7.7%, 2023 +14.3%, 2024 +17.8% vs S&P 500 TR -18.1%, +26.3%, +25.0% (snippet, unverified) | 9 |

**Closing-hour momentum is the strongest published intraday signal.** Baltussen et al. find that the return from the prior close to 30 minutes before the close predicts the last half-hour's return, with out-of-sample R2 of 2.88%, a timing Sharpe of 1.73 on an equal-weighted equity-futures portfolio and no cost model ([JFE 2021 text](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)). The authors tie it to gamma hedging by option market makers and leveraged ETFs: the coefficient grows when dealers' net gamma is more negative. The 0DTE-era evidence does not rule it out but complicates it: Adams et al. find market-maker net gamma predicts order-flow reversals within about ten minutes, fading after an hour ([Quantpedia summary of SSRN 5641974](https://quantpedia.com/do-sp500-0dtes-options-increase-market-volatility/?a=6080)), and Dim, Eraker and Vilkov find no evidence that 0DTE gamma amplifies index volatility ([summary](https://harbourfrontquant.substack.com/p/impact-of-zero-dte-options-on-the)). I read both as suggesting the mechanism may be weaker on average days and concentrated in negative-gamma, high-volatility days, but no paper tests it (inference). The edge per trade is a few basis points over 30 minutes. A single ATM option round trip of 1-3 bp of the underlying (a dated vendor estimate from a search snippet, not rigorous, no URL retained) puts edge-to-cost near or below 1, so the notes' verdict stands: do not use options for this signal. Use ES/MES or SPY shares.

**The trend and volatility gate is the only family with a century of evidence, but the equity-only subset is weak.** The AQR hypothetical figures include simulated transaction costs and fees, yet the equity-index part is a small slice of a multi-asset futures portfolio, and the 2009-2019 decade was poor in real-money index data ([AQR paper](https://oxfordstrat.com/coasdfASD32/uploads/2016/03/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing.pdf); [CME](https://cmegroup.com/education/articles-and-reports/the-market-environment-for-trend-following.html)). Faber's S&P 500 rule shows max drawdown -50.3% vs -83.5% with 3-4 trades a year, but gross ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/long-term-outperformance-from-trends-defined-by-moving-averages)), and Zakamulin's net test finds a Sharpe gain of only 7-12%, possibly fatter left tails, and rules that trail buy-and-hold in about 80% of the intervals between exit and re-entry ([CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/net-performance-of-sma-and-intrinsic-momentum-timing-strategies)). His 0.5% one-way cost overstates today's SPY cost (judgement). Volatility management has mixed support: across 103 strategies the real-time managed version usually does not beat unmanaged ([Cederburg et al., JFE 2020](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)), but the managed market portfolio is the best survivor, and de-risking when volatility is high needs no leverage. So the gate is a risk overlay, not an alpha source. Its job is to switch sleeves off.

**Premium selling has the best structural rationale and the worst tail.** Cboe's PUT index (monthly cash-secured ATM put, T-bills) ran from mid-1986 at 10.13% a year vs 9.85% for the S&P 500, with standard deviation 10.16% vs 15.26% ([Hedgeweek on Cboe study](https://www.hedgeweek.com/options-indices-offered-noteworthy-returns-over-30-years-says-study/)). Its skew is -2.09 and beta 0.56, and Cboe's own research notes the results are gross, not net ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/white-paper-shows-volatility-risk-premium-facilitated-higher-risk-adjusted-returns-for-put-index/)). The 2006-2015 worst drawdowns were -32.7% for PUT and -24.2% for the weekly WPUT ([Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/new-cboe-study-examines-weekly-and-monthly-sp-500-putwrite-indexes/)). The premium is real: VIX averaged 19.3 vs 15.1 realized from 1990-2018, and the variance risk premium predicts returns ([Bollerslev, Tauchen and Zhou, RFS 2009](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=948309)). The harvest is unproven net of spreads: in the Vilkov et al. out-of-sample 0DTE test, iron condors had a net Sharpe of -0.20 after half-spread plus 0.5 bp fees even though the gross Sharpe was 0.77 (fetched through a summarizer; verify against the paper, [repo notes](https://github.com/vilkovgr/0dte-strategies/blob/main/docs/paper/paper-annotated.md)). Vendor win rates of 63-75% mean nothing without profit factor, since a high win rate with defined risk implies the average loser exceeds the average winner. Retail buyers, the other side, are the ones who lose. Israelov and Nielsen add that covered-call-style premium selling is short volatility plus an uncompensated equity-reversal exposure ([FAJ](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Journal-Article/FAJ-Covered-Call-Strategies-One-Fact-and-Eight-Myths.pdf)).

**The rest has weak or negative evidence.** The noise-area SPY strategy reports a Sharpe of 0.61 for its base version and 1.33 with volatility scaling at up to 4x leverage, over 2007-2024, net of $0.0035 per share commission and $0.001 slippage, trading about 1.8 times a day ([SFI WP 24-97](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)). It is a working paper with design changes made after seeing base results, and the independent replications I found were anecdotal. Turn-of-month shows 7.2% a year and Sharpe 1.04 in a 1926-2005 backtest ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/turn-of-the-month-in-equity-indexes)), but a Fed working paper found the effect gone after 1990 in S&P futures ([Atlanta Fed](https://www.atlantafed.org/research/publications/wp/2000/11.aspx)). Pre-FOMC drift essentially disappeared after 2015 ([Kurov, Wolfe and Gilbert](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v40y2021ics1544612320315956.html)). Overnight drift is real in-sample, but daily round trips make costs erase it, and an ATM option held overnight pays theta and spread every night for a drift of roughly 0.03-0.05% (inference, from [Cliff, Cooper and Gulen via CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/buy-at-the-close-and-sell-at-the-open)). Connors-style RSI(2) was tested by its originator with no data-snooping control or friction analysis ([CXO review](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/a-few-notes-on-short-term-trading-strategies-that-work/)), and a 2016-2026 blog test of the long and short rule returned 1.7% CAGR vs 13.5% buy-and-hold ([Backtrex](https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/connors-rsi-2-sp-500)).

## The shortlist: four bots to build, ranked, with exact rules

The ranking combines the rubric score with feasibility on a paper account. Every rule below is a pre-registration draft: parameters come from the papers where stated, and where they are mine they are marked (judgement). Each extra variant you add increases N in the deflated Sharpe, so freeze these and test each as written. Use SPY, QQQ and IWM as separate instruments but treat them as one correlated trial family, not three independent confirmations. The evidence is almost entirely SPY or S&P 500, with QQQ and IWM supported only by Gao et al.'s statement that Nasdaq and Russell 2000 ETFs also show intraday momentum, often stronger out of sample, without extracted tables.

**Rank 1: closing-hour momentum, shares or micro futures (score 12).** Signal at 15:30 ET: r_ROD = last price at 15:30 / prior close - 1. Go long if r_ROD > 0 and short if r_ROD < 0, entering at the next 1-minute bar after a 5-second delay, exiting at 15:59 (a limit order near the close or a MOC order; the papers exit at the close). Baseline is sign-only, as published. One pre-registered variant only: trade only when the previous VIX close is above its trailing 252-day median, based on the Gao finding that the signal is stronger on high-volatility days (judgement for the median cutoff). Instrument: SPY, QQQ or IWM shares, or MES/MNQ/M2K futures, not options. For each signal day, log the quote of the ATM and 1% OTM 0DTE option at entry and exit so the shadow option P&L and cost-to-edge ratio can be measured without trading it. Expected cost: roughly 1-2 bp round trip in shares; the original study used 1.2 bp proportional spread. Decay risk: high, since the mechanism is widely published and post-May-2020 persistence is untested. Planning Sharpe: 1.73 times 0.4 is about 0.69 for the published portfolio, and a single index will be lower; at 0.69 the required track record is about 7 years (derived).

**Rank 2: monthly trend and volatility gate (score 11), used mainly to permit or block the other sleeves.** At the last trading day of each month, risk-on if the ETF's month-end close is above its 10-month simple average of month-end closes (Faber rule), otherwise off. Exposure scaling: weight = min(1, 10% / 21-day realized annualized volatility) (judgement for the 10% target; no leverage, since the Cederburg critique and cost drag bite on levering up). Instrument for the primary test: shares, matching the evidence. Options form, test only after the share version works: a deep-ITM call (delta at least 0.80) with 9-12 months to expiry as a share substitute, rolled at 3 months; no source quantifies its cost versus shares, and the carry and implied-volatility drag are unmeasured (inference). Expected trades: 3-4 a year per ETF, so costs are trivial but the signal cannot be validated by paper trading; test on 15+ years of history including 2008, 2018, 2020 and 2022. Use the gate on every other bot: block premium selling and directional entries when risk-off. Expect the benefit to appear as a smaller drawdown, with Sharpe roughly flat after costs.

**Rank 3: regime-gated put credit spread at 30-45 DTE (score 9; only family with an options-native rationale).** Entry on the first trading day after each monthly expiration, only if the Rank 2 gate is risk-on and 21-day realized volatility is below twice its trailing 252-day median (judgement). Sell a put at about 20 delta and buy the put 3% of spot lower (judgement; the evidence brackets this between PUT's ATM and Spintwig's roughly 5-delta puts at 2-3 DTE, [ERN part 5](https://earlyretirementnow.com/2020/06/17/passive-income-through-option-writing-part-5)). Exit at 50% of maximum profit (the exit Spintwig used) or at 21 DTE, whichever comes first. Size so maximum loss on any spread is at most 1% of account equity and total open maximum loss is at most 3% (judgement). The instrument is a defined-risk credit spread, never a naked put, because naked short premium fails hard gate G4 without a modeled stress period. The reason for 30-45 DTE rather than 0DTE: Volos's snippet puts relative spreads at about 2% for 21-48 DTE against about 9% for ultra-short options, and fewer trades dilute the per-trade cost (low confidence). Cost: a two-leg spread pays the spread four times per round trip (two to enter, two to leave); compute cost as a percentage of credit and of maximum risk, and flag any structure where cost exceeds 25% of the credit (judgement). Expected outcome: positive skew-adjusted Sharpe is not established; expect negative skew, and require the stress-period test before any promotion. The Vilkov et al. conditional results (put ratio spread net Sharpe 0.93) rely on signal selection and an uncovered leg, so do not copy them.

**Rank 4: once-a-day noise-area breakout, shares first, debit spread second (score 9).** Noise area: at each half-hour boundary starting 10:00, the band around the open is the open plus or minus the average absolute move from open to that time over the prior 14 days (volatility multiplier 1), as in the SFI paper. Enter on the first close outside the band, long above, short below, at most one entry a day; exit at the earlier of 16:00 or a close back inside the band or through VWAP. Run exposure at 1x with no leverage, since the 1.33 headline Sharpe depends on volatility scaling with 4x leverage. The paper trades about 1.8 times a day, so this once-a-day version is already a deviation and needs its own testing. Promote the options form only if the shares version nets Sharpe above 0.5 at 2x costs and the average underlying move on trades is at least 3x the round-trip cost of a defined-risk 1DTE ATM-to-+0.5% debit vertical (judgement for width). With a hit ratio of about 37-43% the long-premium alternative pays theta on most days, and implied volatility rises on exactly the high-VIX days where the strategy works best (inference).

**Research only, do not build yet.** Conditional 0DTE defined-risk structures (score 8) need independent reproduction from the public repo on SPX data and then on SPY, QQQ and IWM quotes; the iron fly and condor already lose after costs, and the profitable ratio spreads and strangles carry uncovered risk. Turn-of-month (score 7) is the cheapest to run, about 12 entries a year, but the evidence conflicts and no net post-2005 test exists. If you test it, hold from the last trading day's close through the third trading day of the new month (per the Quantpedia description; the exact day count in the notes is ambiguous) in shares, and only consider a monthly call debit spread afterward.

**Discard.** Bare opening-range breakouts, VWAP/EMA scalps, long ATM premium scalps, naked short premium, gap-fill, pre-FOMC drift, overnight-only option holds, and RSI(2)/IBS as stand-alone entries all fail a hard gate or score under 5 on the rubric. If you want them as a control set, run them through the same harness as placebos.

## Validation plan: long history decides, paper trading falsifies

Because minimum track-record length forces a choice between cost (too many trades) and statistical power (too few), the plan stages evidence by what each stage can prove. Paper trading can detect a cost-model error or an execution failure within 60-90 days, but it cannot confirm a Sharpe of 1-1.5, which needs roughly 2.6-4.1 years at 95% confidence even before the 0.4 haircut (derived from the table above).

Stage 0 is infrastructure, built once: time-stamped historical NBBO option quotes (reject any dataset with daily-close quotes), a fill model charging 50% and 100% of the quoted spread per leg per side, exchange and regulatory fees per contract (the notes could not source current fee schedules), a 1-5 second signal delay, and stop-fill slippage. Add the statistics: PSR, MinTRL with measured skew and kurtosis, DSR with a recorded trial count and a clustering-based estimate of effective independent trials, which the notes could not source, so treat DSR as approximate. Add a placebo harness like the one you already built: at least 1,000 random-direction and random-time versions of each bot on the same quotes and costs, reporting where the real bot sits in that distribution.

Stage 1 is a long historical test of the signal on the underlying (shares or futures, 10-plus years where available), splitting any pre-publication from post-publication segment and reporting the post-publication result separately, since McLean-Pontiff decay applies to it. The sample must cover at least two volatility regimes including a stress event (Feb 2018, Mar 2020, Aug 2024). Stage 2 is walk-forward: for daily or monthly signals use a rolling window of five years in-sample and one year out-of-sample, stepping annually; for the intraday ones use 12 months in-sample and 3 months out-of-sample, stepping quarterly (judgement on window lengths). Fit nothing in the out-of-sample blocks and count every in-sample parameter choice in N.

Stage 3 is cost stress. The bot must stay profitable (positive mean, Sharpe above 0.5) at 2x modeled costs, and gross expectancy should be at least 3x the 1x round-trip cost (both judgement rules from the notes). Also run 3x costs and report how far the result degrades. For option expressions, report cost as a percentage of premium or credit and of maximum risk, not just in dollars per trade.

Stage 4 sets minimum evidence before any promotion. For a trade-based test, t above 3 needs n of at least (3 / per-trade Sharpe)^2 independent trades: 225 trades at a per-trade mean/standard deviation of 0.2, and 900 at 0.1 (derived). Overlapping trades in the same regime do not count as independent. Require measured-skew MinTRL for the Sharpe you observe at 95% confidence, DSR above 0.95, and at least 300 independent trades or the MinTRL, whichever is longer. For the monthly gate and the put-spread sleeve, at 12 or fewer trades a year, only the long historical test can meet that standard, so state plainly that paper trading will not.

Stage 5 is the paper run: at least 60 trading days, or 3 months, with every order logged against its modeled fill. Falsify if realized cost per trade is more than 25% above the modeled cost (judgement), if the bot falls below the 5th percentile of its own placebo distribution, or if it breaches a pre-set loss limit. Even a clean paper run only licenses a reduced-size extended trial, not trust. The paper account must also respect your repo rule that this stays paper or sandbox only.

## Low-confidence items and what is still unknown

The notes disclose these limits and I carry them forward. All formulas for MinTRL, PSR and DSR come from secondary reproductions, not the original papers, and the exact minimum backtest length equation was not verified. The replication-rate literature rests on abstracts and summaries, and no source gives an anomaly survival rate for index-option or intraday strategies. The Gao numbers come from a 2015 working-paper draft hosted on a third-party mirror, which may differ from the JFE version, and QQQ and IWM coefficients were not extracted. The Baltussen result has no cost model and no test after May 2020, including the 2022 and 2024-25 periods; I found no year-by-year table for any of the intraday papers beyond 2020. The ORB replication is a blog, the noise-area replications are anecdotal, and the Backtrex and Quantified Strategies figures are commercial or single-site posts. The Cboe PUT 2022-2024 calendar returns came from a search snippet citing an S&P DJI PDF and should be verified before use, and Sharpe, drawdown and 2008, 2018, 2020 and 2022 behavior for BXM, PUT, WPUT and CLL were not retrieved. The Vilkov et al. 0DTE numbers were read through a summarizer and must be checked against the paper. The Volos spread figures and the Concretum SPX median ATM spread claim ($20 per contract) come from snippets with different bases (relative vs absolute). The notes found no rigorous measurement of SPY, QQQ or IWM 1DTE ATM spreads as a percentage of premium, no options-implemented backtest of any shortlist signal, no 1DTE-specific study, no sourced profit factor, drawdown or cost assumptions for Spintwig, tastylive or Option Alpha, and no sourced loss figures for short-dated sellers in Feb 2018, Mar 2020 or Aug 2024. No peer-reviewed success rate for retail algorithmic bots exists in the notes; prop-firm pass rates are industry-reported and say little about strategy quality. The rubric weights, the 0.4 Sharpe haircut, the 3x-cost rule and the fill-model percentages are design choices, not validated standards.

## Conclusion

The result changes the question. The evidence says retail loses in index options through cost and frequency, not through any lack of cleverness in the entry signal, so the useful design variable is trades per year and cost per trade, not indicator choice. The best-supported signals are those that can pay a one-to-two basis point round trip, and that is a shares and futures cost structure. Options are justified only where the position itself carries a premium (put spreads harvesting the variance risk premium) or where a convex payoff matches a rare large move (a once-a-day breakout), and both claims still lack a net-of-cost test on SPY, QQQ or IWM quotes. Building the cost harness, the placebo distribution and the shadow-quote logger first is therefore worth more than any of the bots themselves, because it turns every later idea into a cheap falsification.

The second implication is about time. At realistic Sharpe ratios of 0.4 to 0.7, honest confirmation takes 7 to 16 years of data, so the plan cannot rely on a short paper run to prove any of these bots. The sensible target for the next quarter is to kill weak ideas fast, hold the survivors at small size, and accept that the most likely outcome for every shortlisted bot, given decay and the evidence base, is a modest or zero net edge.
