## What was established

**Cost.** Option delta is ~0.41 (market fit, 567,242 option-bar minute-pairs). Measured median
full spreads over 28 paper days: QQQ $0.020, SPY $0.010, IWM $0.020. In ATR units the
round-trip cost is **QQQ 0.094 R, SPY 0.079 R, IWM 0.313 R**, pooled 0.196 R. The pooled
`COST_R = 0.2` in `backtest/filters.py` was right on average and wrong per ticker.

**IWM is unaffordable at our size.** Same two-cent spread as QQQ, but a third of the ATR. Four
independent tests agree: worst net in every exit configuration, net negative in every hunt cell
that cleared on other tickers, best raw edge and worst result after cost.

**Exits are not the bottleneck.** 38 exit configurations over 250 days; none profitable after
measured cost. The reason is in `entry-quality`: MFE and MAE over 30 minutes are +2.10 and
−2.13 ATR median. Symmetric excursions cannot be turned into expectancy by any exit rule.

**The triggers carry no directional information.** 11,243 signals, forward returns negative at
every horizon from 1 to 30 minutes, drift-adjusted. The five strongest results across seven
entry-filter variants are all negative.

**No state predicts direction, in either feature family.** 135 price/volatility cells and 30
option-greek cells. Everything that passed a pooled test failed a stability test — 6 of 13
months, or 11 of 21 days. The recurring artifact is daily regime: features that measure "the
market is falling today" predict falling, and reverse on up days.

**Correction made.** `Settings.half_spread` was SPY 0.01 / QQQ 0.015 / IWM 0.02 as *half*
spreads. Measured full spreads are 0.010 / 0.020 / 0.020, so half-spreads are
**0.005 / 0.010 / 0.010**. Changed in `scalpcity/config.py` here and in `~/scalp-city-ema5`.
This is a measurement correction to fill modelling, not a strategy change.

## Void — do not reuse

- **COST_R ≈ 0.55 R**, from the first premium model fitted on Nick Fischer's Discord fills.
  A quarter of his round trips open and close inside one minute, which forces their whole
  premium change into the intercept. Our own data gives 0.196 R. The claim that the exit grid
  was optimistic followed from this and is withdrawn.
- **The first `entry_hunt.py` run**, which produced t-statistics near +39. `atr_rel` and `gap`
  divided by the day's median ATR, which is not known at entry. Fixed to use the prior day.
  Any figure from before that fix is void.

## Tested and rejected, so nobody repeats them

- Nick Fischer's give-back-half trail at his own parameters (stop 10 ATR, arm 4 ATR, give 0.5):
  t = +0.10, and it cuts trade count 81% by holding 159 minutes.
- His 10 ATR "safety stop" is not a stop. His own panel prices it at total loss of premium.
- Trading QQQ only. QQQ is the *weakest* of the three tickers on raw expectancy in four of five
  exits and is not the cheapest. SPY carries what edge exists.
- Seven entry-filter variants: buffer 0.2, buffer 0.4, trend align, chop-all, two-closes, and
  two combinations. All make short-horizon drift more adverse, not less.

## Not tested

- **0 DTE and 2 DTE.** We trade 1 DTE; so does he (his posted rule is 1–3 trading days, and
  the only expiries in his feed are 1 DTE). Testing other expiries needs option chains we have
  not fetched. It changes the payoff transform, not the signal.
- **$2 ITM strikes.** His spec; ours is ATM. Higher delta would lower `COST_R` in R terms. This
  reduces the hurdle; it does not create edge.
- **Longer horizons.** Everything tested is 1–30 minutes.
- **Other edge sources** — scheduled events, overnight gaps, cross-asset.
- **Per-ticker costs inside the live engine.** The grid now uses them; `filters.py` still has
  the single pooled constant.

## Open decisions, for a session that is not the end of a long day

1. Drop IWM. The cost evidence is strong and consistent. It reduces the loss rate; it does not
   create an edge.
2. Whether the 1-minute directional premise is worth continuing at all, given four independent
   tests against it.
